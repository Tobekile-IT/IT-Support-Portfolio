# Case #001 — Desktop Powers On but No Display

**Case type:** Real-world independent IT support | **Status:** Referred to previous repair provider  
**Environment:** Customer site | **Device:** Desktop PC

## Incident summary

The customer reported that the desktop powered on but produced no display on the monitor.

Before travelling to the site, the information available was limited to the reported power/no-display symptom. I brought a spare VGA cable so the existing display connection could be tested as one possible cause.

## Objective

Determine why the desktop powered on without producing display output, test reasonable initial causes, and identify an appropriate next action.

## Investigation and evidence

1. **Initial hypothesis — display connection:** Based on the information available before the visit, a faulty VGA cable or display connection was one possible cause.

2. **VGA cable test:** I tested the system using a replacement VGA cable. The PC continued to power on without producing display output.

3. **Internal hardware inspection:** I opened the desktop, removed and reseated the RAM, and visually inspected the internal hardware and connections. The no-display condition remained.

4. **Additional device history:** Further discussion with the customer revealed that the PC had previously suffered electrical damage, had been repaired by another provider, worked for a period after the repair, and later failed again.

This additional history significantly changed the troubleshooting context.

## Resolution / escalation

The exact hardware fault was not confirmed during the visit.

Because the machine had previously suffered electrical damage and had already been repaired by another provider, I stopped further invasive troubleshooting.

I recommended that the customer first contact the previous repair provider to determine whether the earlier repair was still covered before additional work was performed.

## Outcome and verification limits

**Outcome:** Referred to previous repair provider pending repair/warranty assessment.

The VGA cable was tested and RAM was reseated without restoring display output. The exact failed component was not isolated during the visit.

The previous electrical damage is relevant device history, but it does not by itself prove the cause of the current no-display condition.

## Lessons learned

- Gather previous fault and repair history as early as possible.
- Treat an initial hypothesis as something to test, not a diagnosis.
- Change the troubleshooting approach when new evidence becomes available.
- Previous repair work can affect the appropriate escalation path.
- An unresolved case should be documented as unresolved rather than assigning an unconfirmed root cause.
- Knowing when to stop troubleshooting can be as important as knowing what to test next.
